Essential cow & buffalo care

Good cattle care combines nutrition, housing, hygiene, comfort and regular health observation.

🌿

Balanced nutrition

Provide suitable roughage, green fodder and other feed according to the animal's age, production stage and local veterinary or nutrition advice.

💧

Fresh water

Keep clean drinking water available and make sure troughs are clean and easy for animals to access.

🏠

Clean shelter

Use dry, well-ventilated housing with good drainage, shade and enough space for animals to stand and rest comfortably.

🧼

Hygiene

Regularly remove manure, keep bedding clean and maintain clean milking equipment and udder areas.

🩺

Health monitoring

Watch appetite, rumination, movement, breathing, manure, body condition and unusual behaviour. Arrange veterinary care when needed.

❤️

Gentle handling

Handle cattle calmly and safely. Avoid unnecessary hitting, shouting, overcrowding and stressful restraint.

Feeding & water

Feed requirements vary with breed, age, pregnancy, lactation, body condition and workload.

🌾 Daily feeding basics

  • Offer clean, good-quality fodder and appropriate roughage.
  • Use a balanced ration rather than relying on a single feed.
  • Introduce feed changes gradually.
  • Keep feed areas clean and protect feed from mould and contamination.
  • For dairy animals, seek professional advice for ration formulation.

💦 Water & heat care

  • Provide frequent access to clean drinking water.
  • Offer shade and good airflow during hot weather.
  • Watch for excessive panting, weakness or reduced feed intake.
  • Buffaloes especially benefit from effective heat-management arrangements.
  • Seek veterinary help if an animal shows signs of heat stress.

Simple daily routine

A quick routine can help you notice problems early.

🌅

Morning check

Observe appetite, alertness, walking, breathing and any visible injury or swelling.

🥛

Milking hygiene

Use clean hands and equipment, keep the milking area hygienic and watch for abnormal milk or udder changes.

🌙

Evening check

Confirm water, feed and shelter are available and look for changes in behaviour or rumination.

When to call a veterinarian

Urgent professional help may be needed for severe injury or bleeding, difficulty breathing, collapse, inability to stand, suspected poisoning, severe bloat, prolonged labour, or sudden serious illness.
